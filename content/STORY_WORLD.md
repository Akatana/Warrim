# The Lamp Truce — the story of the war, and of the one who walks between

*An original story for the whole world of this project, written to sit above the valley story in [STORY_NORTHSHIRE.md](STORY_NORTHSHIRE.md). Every zone below has its own small story; each is a chapter of this one. Known characters of the original game (Thrall, Varian, Cairne, Tyrande, Sylvanas, Magni, Jaina) appear only as distant weather: the people who carry the quests are new, and small.*

## 1. The war as it stands

The year is the last of the long peace, and nobody calls it that. The Third War is a few years old. The Horde has a home in Durotar and friends in Mulgore and the Barrens; the Alliance has lost its northern kingdoms and kept its southern ones; the night elves have woken and the Forsaken have not gone away. No army is marching. No one has declared anything.

And yet everywhere the two sides touch, something is always burning:

- At the **Hillsbrad foothills** two towns, Southshore and Tarren Mill, sit a day's ride apart and raid each other's herds.
- In **Ashenvale** the night elves cut down the Horde's loggers, and the Horde's loggers cut down the trees.
- In the **Barrens** caravans disappear; in **Redridge** and **Westfall** the roads close; on the **Arathi Highlands** two garrisons stare at each other across the old Wall of Thoradin.
- Each side has its reasons, and every one of them is *true*: the dead are real, the burned farms are real, the grudges are older than the people who hold them.

What makes this a war and not a quarrel is not hatred. It is **momentum**: every incident is answered, every answer is an incident, and every night someone is surprised in the dark by a thing that "must have been" the other side's work.

### The thing everyone has forgotten

Two hundred years ago, when the Arathi and the Amani trolls had bled each other white, a handful of people from every borderland (human lamplighters, dwarf signalmen, elven moon-wardens, Tauren fire-keepers, troll hunters) met at a lamp and agreed on a custom. It was called the **Lamp Truce**:

> *Where a lamp burns on the border, no blade is drawn. Whoever tends it, tends it for all. A lamplighter may cross any line to carry a light, and any hand that harms one has harmed everyone's.*

Twelve lamps were built along the edges of the peoples. Each has a **lens of starglass** (the rare, faintly singing glass that forms in the crystal craters of Un'Goro and the ice of Winterspring), ground from one single sheet cut by the **Order of the Light at Northshire Abbey**, who kept the Compact's book and the pattern of the lenses. The Truce was never a law. It was a *habit*: shepherds slept beneath the lamps, caravans camped in their light, and soldiers who would cut each other down at dawn did not do it at dusk in sight of a flame. The custom outlived the Compact, and it outlived the kingdoms that signed it. The Horde, who never signed anything, inherited it from the trolls and the Tauren; the Alliance from its farmers. It is the reason the war has stayed small.

### The Brass Ledger

The Lamp Truce is bad for business. A calm border sells no swords, no wagon-guards, no armor, no mercenary contracts, no *information*. The **Brass Ledger** is the oldest and quietest trading house in the world: goblins at its heart, but with a human clerk in every Stormwind counting-house, an orc broker in the Valley of Honor, a Forsaken accountant under Undercity. It buys debts, lends coin, hires the Redhand, the Defias, the Syndicate, the Venture Company: all the small gangs the war has made. It does not want the war to be won. It wants it to *continue*.

For years it nudged: a burned barn, a missing caravan, a forged letter. Then its ledgermaster, **Oswin Brasskettle** (a patient goblin who has never raised his voice), found the cheaper way. *Put out the lamps.* A border without a lamp is a border where anything can be done at night, and blamed on the other side by morning. The Ledger had the pattern of the lenses from a stolen copy of the Compact's book. It needed only the lenses themselves. Each one buys, in the weeks after it is taken, a season of incidents: and each incident is an order for iron.

The Ledger is not a demon, and not an empire. It is a hundred clerks who each did one reasonable thing. That is why it is so hard to stop.

## 2. The player: the Lamplighter

The player is **no one**. They arrive in the first valley of their people with a sword and no history, which is exactly why they are the right person: no kingdom owns them, no old grievance has their name on it, no one will notice a stranger asking about a lamp.

What the player does, in one sentence: **follow the dark from lamp to lamp, relight what can be relit, find out who is putting them out, and decide what the light is for.**

- **The Lamplighter's Lantern.** When the Beacon is lit again, Abbot Hollis gives the player the abbey's old pilgrim's lantern, and with it the Compact's rule: *a lamplighter may cross any line to carry a light.* From now on no gate guard of either faction will strip the player's sword at a Truce lamp; the lantern is a small badge, not a power. (In game terms: lamp quests are open to both factions, and the zones of the other side are reachable under the lamp's protection. The Truce makes a story reason for crossing the line.)
- **Never the hero.** The player does not command armies, kill a world-ending villain, or become king. The player fights mostly bandits, beasts and the Ledger's hired hands, because that is who actually puts lamps out. Every great person in the story is busy; the player is the one who happens to be there.
- **The pattern of the story.** In the early zones the player arrives *after* the theft and pieces it together. In the middle zones they arrive *the same night* and catch a thief in the act. In the late zones they arrive *before*, and wait for the Ledger's people in the dark. By the end they are expected.
- **Both sides can be the player's.** The story is written twice at the beginning: an Alliance opening (Northshire and its sister valleys) and a Horde opening (Durotar, Mulgore, Tirisfal), which are mirror images with the same shape. Both lead to the same middle and the same end. A player who starts Horde meets the human lamps as a stranger under the Truce, and the other way round. The reader will see that the war is the same war from both ends.

### The three-step shape of every lamp

Every lamp quest-chain in the world follows the Northshire pattern, so that every zone teaches the same thing in a new voice:

1. **The dark.** A lamp is out. The place is worse for it, and someone local is sure they know who is to blame (the wrong side, the wrong gang).
2. **The trail.** The player does the zone's honest chores, and finds a small thing that does not fit: a page of a ledger, a coin of a foreign mint, a note in a clerk's neat hand. One fact: *the same hand.*
3. **The lens.** The thief is a cornered person, not a monster, and the lens is somewhere cold. Take it back, relight the lamp, and the zone is a little safer, and a little more honest about who its enemy really was.

## 3. How it ends

By the time the player reaches the maximum level the Ledger has taken **eleven** of the twelve lenses, or has been prevented, and the last lies where the Ledger keeps what it values most: in the **Deep Vault** under **Gadgetzan**, the neutral city of the Steamwheedle cartel, where the Ledger's real accounts are kept. Oswin Brasskettle meets the player there, unarmed and courteous, and makes his case: *the war pays. Everyone knows it. Burn the lamps or don't: someone will always sell the iron.* He is not entirely wrong.

The player takes the twelfth lens, ends the Ledger's hold on it, and carries it to the place where the war began: the **Wall of Thoradin** in Arathi, where the Alliance and Horde garrisons stand face to face. At dusk, both sides watching, the player has one lens and one lamp.

- **Light it for both.** The Wall Lamp is relit as the Truce prescribes: tended by no one, trusted by everyone. Both garrisons lower their weapons for one night, then for a second. No treaty is signed. The border is quiet. This is the ending the story wants, and the hardest to do: the player must have earned the trust of both sides' lamp-keepers in the earlier zones (the table in each zone below says whose).
- **Give it to one side.** The Alliance or the Horde keeps the lens as a trophy and a banner, and the lamp burns for one people only. The war goes on, with better light.
- **Keep it.** The player walks away with the lens, and the border is dark. This is a real ending, and the Ledger's.

The epilogue is the same in all three: someone is always lighting a lamp somewhere, and a stranger is always walking toward it.

## 4. Zones

Level ranges are the ones of the original game. *Lamp* is the Truce lamp of the zone, if it has one. *Hand* is the Ledger's hand in the story. *Beat* is what the player learns.

### Act I — the first valleys (levels 1–10)

Six peoples, six mirrored openings. Each teaches the three-step shape in miniature, with a chore and a small mystery. The Alliance and Horde openings are the same story told in two languages.

**Northshire Valley** (1–6, human) — *Lamp 1.* Told in full in [STORY_NORTHSHIRE.md](STORY_NORTHSHIRE.md): the stolen Beacon, the steward in debt to the Redhand, the kobolds' "big shiny candle". *Hand:* the Redhand's debts are held by a Stormwind clerk who is never named, and the torn ledger page carries a small brass seal: a balance scale holding a flame. *Beat:* nobody in the valley knows what the seal means, except the Abbot, who closes his eyes and says *"So it has begun."* He opens the Compact's book, tells the player about the twelve lamps, and gives them the Lantern. That happens in the second half of the valley ("The Scale and the Flame", level 5); the valley ends at level 6 with the proof that the Ledger is digging starglass beneath the abbey's own hill.

**Elwynn Forest** (6–10, human) — *No lamp; the road to the first one.* Goldshire, Stormwind and the Eastvale logging camp. Peasants hear of the Beacon's return and the wolves and Defias bandits thin out; a Stormwind magistrate asks the player to deliver a letter. *Hand:* **Lady Marisol Vane**, a Stormwind noble whose shipping house is a front for the Ledger; she is charming, and sends the player west toward Westfall on a pretext that will make them useful to her. *Beat:* the first sighting of the Ledger as *respectable*.

**Dun Morogh / Coldridge Valley** (1–10, dwarf and gnome) — *Lamp 3 (Thelsamar) is south-east; the valley has its own small signal-lamp.* Coldridge's forge-signal goes dark when the troggs come out of Gnomeregan's tunnels (the gnomes' city is fallen to leper gnomes and the Dark Iron). Magni's dwarves blame the Dark Irons; the Dark Irons deny it. The player finds that the troggs were *herded* by a whistle made of starglass splinters, the sort of thing a Ledger agent buys in bulk. *Hand:* a crate of gnomish blasting powder marked with a clerk's tally. *Beat:* the Ledger sells to the dwarves' enemies as well; it sold the powder that opened the old tunnel.

**Teldrassil / Shadowglen** (1–10, night elf) — *The Moon-lantern of Auberdine is the nearest lamp.* The great tree is sick with corruption, and the Sentinels are stretched. Small, strange thefts: moonstone dust, lantern oil, a silver bowl from the temple. A hunter's cub is lost in the shadows. *Hand:* a bowl of the type used in Moonglade rites turns up in a crate bound for a Darkshore pier. *Beat:* the elves' calm hides fear: they too know a lamp is wanted at Auberdine and that something is moving to take it.

**Durotar / Valley of Trials** (1–10, orc and troll) — *Lamp 7 (Razor Hill).* Mirror of Northshire: the Razor Hill Spirit-fire is kept by an old Darkspear shaman, **Vol'jin's cousin, Zul'Mora**, and one evening it goes out when its glass is cracked and stolen by a quilboar in the pay of a goblin. The orc side of the story: a young warrior arrives at Razor Hill, helps with the scorpids and the boars, and learns that the fire was not "put out by Alliance spies" as the grunts say. *Hand:* the Venture Company's mark on a barrel, and a goblin broker, **Gorlug Tallytusk**, in the Valley of Honor, who lends to the Horde at friendly rates. *Beat:* the story of the Beacon, in orcish: a fire is a promise, and a lamplighter can cross lines.

**Mulgore / Camp Narache** (1–10, Tauren) — *Lamp 8 (Bloodhoof).* The Hearth-flame is tended by the elder hearth-keeper **Mahna Gentlebraid**, who is old and does not sleep. The quilboar and the Venture Company's prospectors are cutting into the plains; the hunting grounds are shrinking. A Tauren hunter is given a task: guard the flame for three nights. *Hand:* the prospectors' contracts are drawn by an unseen "Ledger Factor" who pays in advance. *Beat:* the first sign that the Ledger is *building something on the Horde's side*, a hidden buyer in the Barrens.

**Tirisfal Glades / Deathknell** (1–10, Forsaken) — *Lamp 9 (Brill).* The Forsaken have kept the Grave-lamp lit through all their dead years, because the gravedigger who tends it believes that someone, somewhere, is still being buried. The Scarlet Crusade is burning the fields; the lamp is the only thing in Tirisfal that the Scarlets leave alone. Someone steals its lens from inside Brill. *Hand:* **Mordra Quill**, a Forsaken accountant of the Apothecary Quarter, who laughs and says the lens was *sold*, not stolen. *Beat:* the dead have debts too, and the Ledger lends to them.

### Act II — the first roads (levels 10–25)

The player has the Lantern and a first scrap of the Ledger's hand. They walk from lamp to lamp, arriving *after* each theft. The zones begin to touch, and the war's two sides begin to appear in each other's chapters.

**Westfall** (10–20, Alliance) — *Lamp 2, the Sentinel Lantern, at Sentinel Hill.* A farming country ruined by the **Defias Brotherhood**, ex-stonemasons turned outlaws, who say the crown cheated them. The Lantern has been dark since the harvest. Farmers blame the Defias; the Defias, strangely, say they did not take it. They did not: it was taken by a Brotherhood *splinter group* paid by Lady Vane. *Hand:* a Defias captain with a Stormwind-minted purse. *Lens:* in the Deadmines, behind an engineer who has been told to melt the glass for a ship's lamp. *Beat:* the Brotherhood are victims too; the player will be asked to choose whether to kill them or to take their grievance to the Westfall militia. Either way the Lantern is relit.

**Loch Modan** (10–20, Alliance) — *Lamp 3, the Forge-signal, at Thelsamar.* The dwarves of Thelsamar are fighting the Dark Iron's agents and the troggs, and arguing with the gnomes over the cost. The Forge-signal, a mirror of polished steel that flashes from tower to tower, has lost its lens: the troggs, the dwarves say, but the player finds a **Ledger coin** in a trogg nest. *Hand:* a gnome engineer who sold the lens for a mechanical heart. *Beat:* the guilt of the small. Nobody here is a villain; the engineer is simply tired.

**Darkshore** (10–20, night elf) — *Lamp 6, the Moon-lantern, at Auberdine.* The shore is poisoned by the corruption pouring from the south. The Moon-lantern is out; a murloc-ridden coast, a satyr cult and a secret lodge of goblin salvagers. A dying Sentinel tells the player the lens was taken from the temple by a hooded elf who does not stay in one place. *Hand:* a Ledger factor dressed as a night elf: the first sign the Ledger has agents who can pass for anyone. *Beat:* the player meets the fear of the Kaldorei and is trusted with a Moonglade dusk-stone, to be used later.

**Silverpine Forest** (10–20, Forsaken) — *A Horde-side stop on the road from Brill.* The Forsaken and the worgen-haunted Shadowfang Keep. The player helps the Forsaken with a Scarlet raid and finds that the worgen's sudden rampages have been *driven* by a Ledger-supplied lure. *Hand:* a clerk's brass whistle at a worgen lair. *Beat:* the Ledger feeds beasts into a border and calls it nature.

**The Barrens** (10–25, Horde) — *Lamp 10, the Crossroads Fire.* The Crossroads is the Horde's great crossroads and the Barrens' great problem: caravans vanish, quilboar raid, centaurs raid, and the Venture Company mines the hills for gold. The Crossroads Fire was lit by a Tauren caravan-mistress, **Shara Dustwalker**, and dies the night a caravan is robbed. *Hand:* a Venture foreman called **Sprocket Quillfuse** with a ledger far too tidy for a mine. *Lens:* in Fray Island, where Ledger-hired quilboar have been paid in iron. *Beat:* the Barrens show the war from the Horde's side and confirm the pattern: a lamp dies, a caravan is robbed, a side is blamed. The first time the player *prevents* a theft: they spot the thief before he leaves.

**Redridge Mountains** (15–25, Alliance) — *Lamp 4, the Watchlamp, at Lakeshire.* The orc remnants of the Blackrock clan raid the mountain roads; the Lakeshire Watch is under siege. The Watchlamp, tended by a widower named **Corporal Aldous Fenn**, is dark, and the Blackrock raids have doubled. *Hand:* the Blackrock chieftain is paid in Alliance steel. The player meets a Blackrock orc who offers a swap: his sick child for the player's quiet. *Beat:* the first time the player is offered a good reason to take the Ledger's money. They can refuse.

**Ashenvale** (18–30, contested) — *No Truce lamp of the twelve; a lamp of a different kind.* The night elves and the Horde's Warsong lumberers fight over the trees. The Horde's Splintertree Post and the elves' Astranaar both want the lamp that used to stand at the old border. The player carries a letter from each commander to the other, and discovers that someone has been *forging* the commands on both sides to keep the fighting hot. *Hand:* the forger's table, with a Ledger stamp, in a hut by Lake Falathim. *Beat:* the war could be ended by a sheet of paper that is true.

**Stonetalon Mountains** (15–27, contested) — The Venture Company's strip-mine and the Horde-Alliance quarrel over the Charred Vale. A goblin engineer **Tink Brasswhistle** has built a flying contraption for the Ledger; she changes sides when she sees what it is for. *Hand:* the aircraft is a *lens delivery* machine. *Beat:* the Ledger is moving lenses by air.

### Act III — the same night (levels 25–40)

The player now catches thieves in the act. The Ledger notices, and begins to send people to talk to them. The faction line starts to blur.

**Duskwood** (18–30, Alliance) — *The town of Darkshire keeps its lamps lit against the dark, and the dark is real.* Night-walking dead, werewolves and ghouls; the Night Watch has been shrinking. The lamps of Darkshire are not Truce lamps but the townsfolk treat them as one. The player is asked to guard one lamp for three nights, and meets **Stitches**, a flesh-golem sold to the Watch by a Ledger factor as a "guard", and gone wrong. *Hand:* the sale receipt. *Beat:* the Ledger's wares are not even good.

**Wetlands** (20–30, Alliance) — *Lamp 5, the Harbor Light, at Menethil Harbor.* The Dark Iron and the Dragonmaw clan fight over the marshes; ships are lost on the rocks. The Harbor Light's lens is gone, and the ships do not know which side's fault it is. *Hand:* a ship's captain who sells his manifests. The player catches the thief in the act at the pier and learns where the lens went: south, to a free port on the sea. *Beat:* Booty Bay is named for the first time.

**Hillsbrad Foothills** (20–30, contested) — *Lamp 11, the Twin Lamps at Southshore and Tarren Mill.* The most important zone in the middle of the story. Two towns across the same valley, a human garrison and a Forsaken-Orc one, and a long list of grievances: stolen cattle, burned mills, a hanged farmer. Each town's lamp is *half* of one lens, cut for the two sides to share. Both are dark. Each town blames the other. The player goes to both. *Hand:* the **Syndicate**, a gang of Alterac exiles, are paid by the Ledger, and have been conducting both sides' raids. *Beat:* the player stands in front of both lamp-keepers and says: *"it was the same people."* Neither believes it. The player must catch the Syndicate with proof both sides can see. When they do, there is a night of quiet. It does not last, but it was the first.

**Stranglethorn Vale** (30–45, neutral) — *Booty Bay: the Ledger's front door.* The Steamwheedle goblins keep the free port neutral, and the Ledger keeps its largest counting-house under the lighthouse. The jungle outside is a nest of trolls, tigers and pirates, but the real story is in the city, where the player can walk into the **Gilded Quill** and be offered a seat. *Hand:* all of it. *Beat:* the player meets **Oswin Brasskettle's** deputy, **Pennywick Gearloose**, who offers a job, a price and a polite warning. The player now knows the name, the seal and the plan: the lamps were stolen *to be put out*, and the glass is kept to be sold to the war's heirs. The player can take the Ledger's coin, or walk out. Walking out starts the chase.

**Desolace** (30–40, contested) — The centaurs raid both the Horde and the night elves; a Horde camp and an elf camp share a fountain and a distrust. *Hand:* a stolen lens-fragment glints in a centaur chief's headdress, traded for iron. *Beat:* the Ledger sells to the "monsters" too, and everyone is its customer.

**Dustwallow Marsh** (35–45, contested) — **Theramore**, Jaina's human harbor, sits by a Horde swamp. The Alliance and the Horde keep an uneasy peace there, and the marsh is the setting for a story of two neighbors: a Theramore captain and a Horde scout who have both lost soldiers to the same raptor-poacher. *Hand:* the poacher's pay is Ledger gold. *Beat:* the first time the player brings a human and an orc to the same table. It works for one evening.

**Alterac Mountains** (30–40, contested, mostly empty) — The ruined kingdom whose lords sold their own people. The Syndicate's mountain hold, a ghost-keep, and an old lamp-house whose lens was taken a generation ago. *Hand:* the Syndicate's ledger-keeper, a woman who deserted the Ledger and is willing to talk. *Beat:* the Ledger has *always* been taking lenses; the Compact's twelve are, in truth, the surviving twelve of twenty.

**Arathi Highlands** (30–40, contested) — *Lamp 12, the Wall Lamp.* A first visit: two garrisons, the Alliance's Refuge Pointe and the Horde's Hammerfall, on either side of the Wall of Thoradin. A battlefield where skirmishes happen at dusk. The Wall Lamp is a ruin and nobody has dared touch it. The player may observe, run errands for either side, and notice that the skirmishes stop whenever a *light* is carried across, a tinker's lantern, a mule driver's torch. *Beat:* what the Truce was, shown in action. The player does not light the lamp yet: they have no lens. They will come back.

**Thousand Needles** (25–35, Horde-leaning) — The Freewind Post, a Tauren settlement, and the Shimmering Flats racing track, run by goblins with a taste for wagering. *Hand:* the Ledger runs a *racing book* and uses it to move lens-glass inside race-cars. *Beat:* the player wins a race that has been fixed and learns what the fixing was for.

**Swamp of Sorrows** (35–45, contested) — The dragonkin and the Atal'ai trolls, a lost temple and a rotting coast. *Hand:* a Ledger-hired sorcerer is bargaining with the Atal'ai for a *different* use of starglass: a ritual lens. *Beat:* the first sign that the lenses can be made to do something other than shine.

### Act IV — before the thief (levels 40–50)

The player now arrives first. They wait in the dark for the Ledger's people, and the Ledger sends someone to wait for them.

**The Hinterlands** (40–50, contested) — The Wildhammer dwarves' Aerie Peak and the Revantusk trolls' Camp, neighbors in an old friendship that the Ledger has been undermining. *Hand:* a faked trade in dragon-eggs. *Beat:* a dwarf and a troll, who have been friends for forty years, are on the verge of killing each other. The player shows them the forgery. It is the second time the player makes peace by pointing at paper.

**Badlands** (35–45, neutral) — The player meets the ruins of the Dark Iron rebellion and a dwarf miner who is the last descendant of a lens-grinder. *Hand:* the Ledger tried to buy her family workshop. She keeps the *pattern of the lenses* in her head and will teach the player to grind. *Beat:* the player can now make a lens, a plain one of ordinary glass, to stand in a lamp's place until the true one is found. A dark lamp is better with a false light than with none.

**Searing Gorge** (43–50, contested) — The Dark Iron dwarves' slave-driving of the Thorium Brotherhood. *Hand:* the Ledger's most guilty customer: the Dark Irons buy iron and sell it again. *Beat:* the Ledger's accounts are balanced here: for every blade sold to one side, a shield to the other. The player sees the *math* of the war.

**Tanaris** (40–50, neutral) — *Gadgetzan, the Steamwheedle's neutral city, above the Deep Vault.* Dunes, pirates, a sunken ship and the Wastewander bandits. The city itself is a place where Alliance and Horde trade in peace, and so a perfect thing to defend: the player's heart is in the place. *Hand:* the vault entrance is not hidden; it is simply expensive. *Beat:* the player learns where the twelfth lens is, and that they cannot open the vault until they have proved to the Steamwheedle that the Ledger has broken the Cartel's neutrality. The player needs the Cartel's **Baron** on their side.

**Feralas** (40–50, contested) — The Horde's Camp Mojache and the Alliance's Feathermoon Stronghold. A drowned elven city and the ogres of Dire Maul. *Hand:* the Ledger has been selling ogres cannons. *Beat:* a lamp-keeper from each side is lost in the same storm, and the player gets them both home. They owe the player a favor each.

**Azshara** (45–55, Horde-leaning) — The ruins of the high elves and a goblin stockade. A **Ledger shipyard** is here, where the lenses are packed in barrels for the sea. *Hand:* the packing house, with tallies of every lens taken in the world. *Beat:* the player can burn the shipyard. The cost: the barrels hold lenses that are not stolen, but *given*: the Ledger has found people who sell their own lamps for a price. The player learns the Ledger's last lesson: it only works because some people say yes.

**Blasted Lands** (45–55, neutral) — The Dark Portal's scar. A zone of ruin and rust, with a sorrowful tale of a lens the Ledger *sold to the wrong customer*: a warlock circle. *Beat:* a lens can be turned dark too, with a bad ritual. The player must put it right.

**Un'Goro Crater** (48–55, neutral) — *The source of the starglass.* A lost world of crystals and dinosaurs. The Ledger has put a mining crew in the crater to take the glass at its source, and not just the lenses. Here the player meets a **Titan-keeper construct** whose job was to hold the crystal fields for all peoples. *Beat:* the starglass sings when a lamplighter is near, and goes silent for the Ledger's men. It is not the glass that makes a lens a Truce lens: it is who carries it.

### Act V — the long count (levels 50–60)

The player knows who, why and where. Their task is to gather the allies they have earned and go to the Wall.

**Burning Steppes** (50–58, neutral) — The black dragons' mountain. The Ledger's biggest customer is here, the Blackrock orcs and the Black Dragonflight, and it has been selling a *lens of fire* to the spire. The player finds out that the war has also been bought by something older than either faction. *Beat:* the Truce is the thing the Dragonflight fears; a quiet border is a border where heroes can meet.

**Western Plaguelands** (51–58, neutral) — The Argent Dawn holds the land against the Scourge. The lamp-house of a ruined chapel at Chillwind Camp has a lens the Order of the Light had hidden. *Hand:* the Ledger sells to the Scourge, and the Scourge pays in bodies. *Beat:* the player is offered an alliance of the dead and the living. The Argent Dawn accepts the player into the cause of the Truce.

**Eastern Plaguelands** (53–60, neutral) — Light Hope Chapel, where the Argent Dawn's last stronghold stands. Here the player learns the fate of the Order of the Light: its other mother house is lost, and the Compact's book is incomplete. *Beat:* the book names the Wall Lamp's lens as the key to all the others.

**Felwood** (48–55, contested) — The corrupted forest. A night-elf druid and a Horde shaman cleanse a spring together, because they must. *Beat:* they will remember the player at the Wall.

**Winterspring** (53–60, neutral) — *The ice-fields where starglass also forms.* The Everlook goblins mine it on contract. The Ledger has a final counting-house here. *Hand:* the ledgermaster's own accounts, which show that Oswin Brasskettle owes a debt to someone else. *Beat:* the Ledger has a creditor. The creditor is not the player's concern. Not this time.

**Silithus** (55–60, neutral) — The Qiraji's desert. A war against something that is not human at all. *Beat:* the Truce was never meant only for the two factions. The Cenarion Circle and the Horde, the Alliance and the Brood of Nozdormu stand at one lamp for the first time. The player can help. Silithus is the first time the player sees the Truce *work at scale*.

**Moonglade** (55–60, neutral) — The druids' sanctuary. The player returns the Moonglade dusk-stone from Darkshore, and Archdruid **Hamuul Runetotem** blesses the Lantern. The Lantern can now light *any* lamp.

**Deadwind Pass** (55–60, neutral) — The player passes through Karazhan's shadow on the way to the Swamp. A short chapter, in which the Ledger's last factor is met: **Pennywick Gearloose**, who says the vault is open: *he has left the key, because he wants the player to try.*

### The great cities

The cities are the world's own: they carry no lamp, but each holds a **voice** that matters at the end.

- **Stormwind** — Lady Marisol Vane's house. Once the player has proof, the Alliance leader's court hears it, or does not. Varian's regent is not deceived and not helpful.
- **Ironforge** — Magni's court. The dwarves of Thelsamar, Coldridge and the Wildhammer speak for the player at the Wall if the player has helped them.
- **Darnassus** — Tyrande's temple. The Moon-lantern of Auberdine is the night elves' badge of trust.
- **Orgrimmar** — the Valley of Honor. Gorlug Tallytusk's counting-house is under the Horde's nose, and Thrall's people burn it when the player brings the evidence.
- **Thunder Bluff** — Cairne's great rise. The hearth-keepers send a delegate to the Wall.
- **Undercity** — Sylvanas's court, and the Apothecary Quarter where Mordra Quill's books are kept. The Forsaken are slow to speak and, at the end, the first to light their lamp.

## 5. Where the game stands, and what each zone needs

Only Northshire is built; the rest of the world is terrain you can walk through. The story is arranged so that it grows by whole zones, in this order:

1. **Northshire** (first ten quests built; the story of the second half, quests 11 to 17, is planned in [STORY_NORTHSHIRE.md](STORY_NORTHSHIRE.md), which also brings the valley to level 6). The hooks to the world are there:
   - The **torn ledger page** (Garrick Thorne's drop) is the first sight of the *balance-scale-and-flame seal*; the Redhand's new clerk, Pell Dunmore, carries the seal itself, and Abbot Hollis recognises it in "The Scale and the Flame".
   - There the Abbot gives the player the **Lamplighter's Lantern** (a key item) and the Compact's first lines. "Glass in the Dark" and "Skorn the Geomancer" show the Ledger digging starglass under the abbey's own hill and shipping the blanks to a warehouse in Stormwind, which sends the player to Elwynn Forest.
2. **Elwynn Forest** (Goldshire, Stormwind road): the Defias, the Lady Vane quest, the road to Westfall. It is the nearest and needs no new map.
3. **Westfall** and **Redridge**: the next two lamps. Both are 10–25, human, and a short walk from Elwynn.
4. **Dun Morogh / Loch Modan**: lamp 3.
5. The Horde mirror, **Durotar**: the same story in the other direction. Do it with `--map Kalimdor --pos -618,-4251`, which is already in the README.
6. The neutral middle (Stranglethorn, Tanaris), the Wall, and the end.

Each zone should be given its own file of the form `STORY_<ZONE>.md` once it is built, in the manner of [STORY_NORTHSHIRE.md](STORY_NORTHSHIRE.md), with a place table, a cast, a quest chain and what the player finds. This file is the bible they follow. When a zone's story differs from the paragraph above, the zone's file wins and this file should be corrected.

## 6. Tone

Small, warm and a little wry. Nobody is a chosen one. The villain is an accountant. The war is real, the grievances on both sides are real, and the thing that could stop it is a lamp on a hill. The player is the person who carries it.
